Anti-VEGF Drugs

These drugs inhibit VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor), a key angiogenic factor that promotes abnormal retinal blood vessel growth (Wang & Lo, 2018). They are given monthly or bimonthly due to their short half-life and risk of endophthalmitis.

Ranibizumab (Lucentis)

Most evaluated and widely tested anti-VEGF medication.

Benefits:

  • Greater BCVA improvement
  • Reduced CRT
  • Comparable to panretinal photocoagulation for PDR

Adverse Effects:

  • Elevation in intraocular pressure
  • Vitreous hemorrhage
  • Inflammation

Pegaptanib (Macugen)

Early FDA-approved anti-VEGF.

Benefits:

  • Better BCVA improvement vs. sham (2-year trial)

Adverse Effects:

  • Conjunctival hemorrhage
  • Elevated intraocular pressure

Aflibercept (EYLEA)

Highly effective, particularly in patients with poor baseline vision.

Benefits:

  • Greater BCVA gains than laser
  • Effective for DME and PDR

Adverse Effects:

  • Elevated intraocular pressure
  • Vitreous hemorrhage
  • Inflammation

Bevacizumab (Avastin) – Off-label

Widely used, cost-effective anti-VEGF option.

Benefits:

  • Better median visual acuity
  • Greater CRT reduction vs. laser
  • Improvement in DME

Adverse Effects:

  • Elevated intraocular pressure
  • Vitreous hemorrhage
  • Inflammation

Anti-Inflammatory Therapy

Intravitreal steroid treatments help reduce inflammation and treat DME.

Triamcinolone – Off-label

Benefits:

  • Visual improvement in DME
  • Better outcomes when combined with prompt laser

Adverse Effects:

  • Cataract formation (often requiring surgery)
  • Elevated intraocular pressure
  • Vitreous hemorrhage

DEX Implant (Ozurdex) – FDA Approved

Benefits:

  • Greater BCVA improvement
  • Significant CRT reduction
  • Long-acting (up to 6 months)
  • Effective for refractory DME

Adverse Effects:

  • Cataract formation
  • Elevated intraocular pressure
  • Vitreous hemorrhage

FAe Insert (Iluvien, 0.2 mg) – FDA Approved

Benefits:

  • Sustained multi-year drug release
  • Improved BCVA over 2 years
  • Lower rate of IOP elevation vs older implants

Adverse Effects:

  • Cataract surgery often needed
  • Elevated intraocular pressure
  • Risk of glaucoma

Laser Treatment

Focal/Grid Laser (for Macular Edema)

Benefits:

  • Reduces risk of moderate vision loss
  • Helps stabilize and improve vision
  • Decreases persistent macular edema
  • Often paired with anti-VEGF to reduce injection burden

Adverse Effects:

  • Mild loss of visual acuity
  • Small blind spots

Panretinal Photocoagulation (PRP) – for PDR

Benefits:

  • Reduces risk of severe vision loss
  • Inhibits disease progression
  • Helpful in high-risk cases (e.g., vitreous hemorrhage)

Adverse Effects:

  • Loss of visual acuity
  • Peripheral visual field constriction
  • Reduced night vision
